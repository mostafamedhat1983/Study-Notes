- **DevOps Definition**: DevOps involves operations and development engineers working together throughout the entire service lifecycle, from design to deployment and production support.
- **Integration of Lean and Agile**: DevOps brings together development, operations, Lean principles, and often Agile practices to improve the flow, reliability, and delivery of software.
- **CALMS**: CALMS is a commonly used DevOps framework: culture, automation, Lean, measurement, and sharing.
- **Holistic Approach**: Lean principles of flow inform the entire software development process, from product conception to operations, emphasizing continuous improvement and value delivery to the customer.

## CALMS means

- **Culture**: DevOps starts with collaboration and trust between development, operations, and related teams.
- **Automation**: Teams automate builds, tests, deployments, and other repeatable work to reduce errors and speed delivery.
- **Lean**: The goal is to reduce waste, shorten feedback loops, and improve flow through the software lifecycle.
- **Measurement**: Teams track metrics and outcomes so they can see whether delivery and reliability are improving.
- **Sharing**: Teams openly share knowledge, documentation, feedback, and lessons learned across development, operations, and other stakeholders. This includes blameless postmortems, where incident learnings are documented and shared to prevent repeat failures.

## The three ways of DevOps

- **Flow**: Optimize the whole system so work moves smoothly from development to operations to the customer, instead of optimizing isolated silos.
- **Feedback**: Shorten and amplify feedback loops so problems are detected early and corrected quickly.
- **Continual learning and experimentation**: Build a culture where teams experiment, learn from failure, and improve daily work over time.

## Simple meaning

The Three Ways mean: deliver work faster, detect issues earlier, and keep learning continuously.

## Good interview answer

“The Three Ways of DevOps are Flow, Feedback, and Continual Learning. Flow improves end-to-end delivery, Feedback helps catch issues early, and Continual Learning builds a culture of experimentation and improvement.”

## Relation to CALMS

CALMS is a commonly used DevOps framework, while the Three Ways focus on principles that drive DevOps behavior and practices. They are related, but they are not the same model.

## DevOps, Lean, and Agile

Lean and Agile are important foundations of DevOps. Agile contributes iterative development and collaboration, Lean contributes flow, systems thinking, and waste reduction, and DevOps applies these principles across the full delivery pipeline, including operations.

## Small distinction

They are related but not identical. Agile, Lean, and DevOps overlap heavily, but each has a different emphasis: Agile centers on development practices, Lean on end-to-end value and efficiency, and DevOps on unifying development and operations for fast, reliable delivery.

## DevOps in different environments

DevOps principles work with Agile, Scrum, Waterfall, and other delivery methods. They also apply to greenfield products, legacy systems, custom software, and packaged or vendor software.

The goal is to improve the parts of the value stream the team can control. Common improvements include reducing handoffs, using smaller batches, automating tests and deployments, and improving collaboration with business, operations, security, and vendors.

## Product ownership

A DevOps team should build, own, operate, and improve its product throughout its lifecycle. This shared ownership improves feedback, reliability, and delivery speed.
