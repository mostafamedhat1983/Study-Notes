## What ITIL is

ITIL originally stood for Information Technology Infrastructure Library, and modern ITIL 4 describes service management in a more holistic way across development, operations, governance, and business value. ITIL 4 includes practices such as incident management, problem management, change enablement, release management, service request management, monitoring and event management, and service level management.

## Why it matters in DevOps

DevOps focuses on speed, automation, collaboration, and continuous delivery, while ITIL helps organizations add structure, risk control, and service reliability. In practice, they are not opposites: change management, release management, incident handling, and feedback loops are areas where DevOps and ITIL often work well together.

## Change enablement and DevOps

Change enablement is about increasing successful changes by understanding risk, authorizing changes appropriately, and keeping a record of what changed.

DevOps improves change enablement through automated builds, tests, deployments, monitoring, and audit trails. Evidence from these controls can replace unnecessary manual paperwork or approvals.

## Risk-based change management

Not all changes have the same risk. Teams with strong automation, low change failure rates, and fast service recovery can safely use lightweight approval processes for routine changes.

Higher-risk changes may need additional review, testing, or rollout controls.

## Freeze windows

A freeze window restricts changes during high-risk or high-revenue periods. It can reduce immediate risk, but it may also create a large batch of delayed changes after the freeze.

A better long-term goal is to build delivery capabilities that allow small, automated, observable, and reversible changes to be released safely at any time.
