- **Blameless Incident Reviews**: A learning-focused review after an incident that examines how the system, process, and conditions allowed the failure to happen.

## Purpose

The purpose is to understand reality, improve the system, and prevent similar incidents. It is not to find someone to blame.

A blameless review encourages people to share accurate information without fear, which improves learning and future reliability.

## Contributing factors

Complex systems rarely have one root cause. Incidents usually result from several contributing factors, such as:

- Gaps in automation
- Unsafe deployment processes
- Missing safeguards or monitoring
- Poor capacity planning
- Conflicting priorities
- High workload or fatigue
- Unclear procedures

## Human error

Human error is a starting point for investigation, not a root cause. Ask what conditions made the action possible, likely, or difficult to recover from.

For example, a wrong production configuration may expose missing review controls, manual changes, confusing access, poor automation, or an unsustainable workload.

## Good review practices

- Use a neutral facilitator.
- Build a shared timeline of what happened.
- Ask open questions and avoid accusations.
- Identify contributing factors and system improvements.
- Assign clear owners and follow up on improvement actions.
- Study successful changes as well as failures.

## Key idea

A strong incident review improves the system and the way teams learn. It does not punish individuals for operating within imperfect conditions.
