Value stream mapping is a Lean technique for visualizing all of the steps required to deliver value to a customer. It shows both the work flow and the information flow so teams can find delays, bottlenecks, handoffs, rework, and unnecessary approvals.

## Current state and future state

- **Current-state map**: Shows how work moves today, including the real delays and constraints.
- **Future-state map**: Describes an improved flow after waste, dependencies, and bottlenecks are reduced.

Start by understanding the current state. Improving a process without seeing the actual work can optimize the wrong part of the system.

## Process time vs waiting time

- **Process time**: Time actively spent working on an item.
- **Waiting time**: Time an item waits in a queue, for an approval, a dependency, an environment, or a decision.

In knowledge work, waiting time is often much larger than process time. Value stream mapping makes this hidden delay visible.

## Lead time vs cycle time

- **Lead time**: Time from a customer request until the customer receives value.
- **Cycle time**: Time from when a team starts actively working on an item until it is delivered.

Lead time reflects the customer experience. Cycle time helps a team improve the part of the flow it directly controls.

## What to look for

A value stream map can reveal:

- Long queues and excessive work in progress.
- Repeated handoffs between specialized teams.
- Dependencies that delay delivery.
- Manual testing, approval, or deployment steps.
- Rework caused by defects or unclear requirements.
- Bottlenecks where demand is greater than capacity.

## Continuous flow

Continuous flow means moving small pieces of work through the system smoothly instead of waiting to release a large batch. Teams support flow by limiting work in progress, reducing batch size, automating repeatable steps, and resolving blockers quickly.

Small batches reduce risk because failures are easier to identify, understand, roll back, and fix. They also create faster feedback from customers and production systems.

## Simple example

A team may discover that coding takes one day but work waits five days for manual testing and another three days for a release window. The main opportunity is not making developers code faster. It is reducing the queues and improving the testing and release process.

## Good interview answer

You can say: “Value stream mapping visualizes the end-to-end path from demand to customer value. I use it to separate active work from waiting time, identify bottlenecks and handoffs, then run small experiments such as WIP limits or deployment automation to improve flow and reduce lead time.”
