- **Unplanned Work**: Work that interrupts planned commitments and was not included in the team's original capacity plan.

## Common sources

- Production incidents and service restoration.
- Urgent defects, patches, or support requests.
- Discovery work, research, and unexpected technical investigation.
- Quick questions that take more time than expected.
- New dependencies or late-breaking complexity.
- Context switching caused by changing priorities.

## Why it matters

Unplanned work reduces the capacity available for planned features, technical debt, and improvement work. It makes delivery dates less predictable and can contribute to burnout.

## How to manage it

- Track unplanned work separately from planned work.
- Use past data to reserve capacity and improve forecasts.
- Reduce incidents through automation, quality practices, and fast feedback loops.
- Limit context switching by setting clear priorities.
- Use loosely coupled architecture so teams can test and deploy independently.

## Key idea

Unplanned work will not disappear completely. The goal is to make it visible, understand its causes, plan for it, and reduce avoidable disruption.
