- **DevOps Organizational Models**: Ways of organizing teams to improve flow, collaboration, ownership, and continuous delivery.

## DevOps-friendly characteristics

A suitable organizational model should:

- Reduce handoffs, wait time, approval gates, and delivery barriers.
- Support fast feedback and open communication across teams.
- Give teams shared accountability for product outcomes.
- Build security, compliance, auditability, and risk controls into delivery.
- Distribute operational responsibilities fairly to avoid burnout.
- Give people close to the work a voice and authority to stop unsafe changes.
- Adapt based on learning and feedback.

## Matrix model

Functional specialists support product teams through informal or dotted-line relationships.

This model can use scarce expertise efficiently, but shared specialists can create priority conflicts. Clear prioritization is needed when several teams need the same skill.

## Embedded model

People from functional teams are dedicated to a product team while their formal reporting structure remains unchanged.

Embedding improves collaboration and product context. It is most useful when a product has a high or strategic need for a specialist skill.

## Product and platform model

Product teams own customer-facing products. Platform teams provide reusable internal services and self-service capabilities.

A platform may provide cloud foundations, deployment tooling, SRE capabilities, security tooling, or shared APIs. Treat the platform as a product with its product teams as customers.

## Adaptive model

Teams and structures can form, change, or retire as organizational needs change.

This model can improve speed to mobilization, but it needs strong alignment and governance. Constant reorganization can reduce stability, quality, and team effectiveness.

## Choosing a model

There is no universal best structure. Start with the outcomes the organization needs, such as faster delivery, reliability, customer value, or cost efficiency.

Use experiments and feedback to improve the model gradually. Organizational change should support culture and flow of value, not replace the work of improving them.
