MVP stands for **Minimum Viable Product**. It is the simplest version of a product that has just enough core features to deliver value, test the idea with real users, and collect feedback for future improvement.

## Simple meaning

An MVP is not a fake demo and not a full product. It is a **working** product with only the essential features needed to solve the main user problem.

## Why teams build it

Teams use an MVP to validate whether users actually want the product before spending too much time and money on extra features. This approach helps reduce risk, speed up learning, and guide future development using real feedback.

## Small batches and learning

An MVP should be delivered as a small batch: a narrow, usable slice of value that can be tested quickly. Small batches reduce the cost of being wrong because the team learns early instead of waiting for a large release.

Teams can use feature flags to release an MVP safely to a limited group of users, observe results, and expand, change, or remove the feature based on evidence.

## MVP vs a full product

An MVP has enough quality to be safe, usable, and valuable for its intended users. It does not include every possible feature, integration, or optimization.

The goal is validated learning, not simply releasing something quickly. A weak or unusable product does not produce reliable feedback.

## Easy example

If you want to build a food delivery app, the MVP might only include signup, restaurant listing, order placement, and payment. Features like coupons, live driver tracking, loyalty points, and advanced analytics can come later.

## Good interview answer

You can say: “An MVP is the smallest functional and usable version of a product that delivers value to early users and generates feedback. I use small batches and feature flags to validate assumptions early, reduce delivery risk, and decide what to build next based on evidence.”
